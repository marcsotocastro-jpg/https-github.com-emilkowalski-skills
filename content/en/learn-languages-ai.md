To learn a language you have to practise it, and the hardest part is usually finding someone to practise with. An AI assistant can be a conversation partner available at any hour, with endless patience and able to adapt to your level. It doesn't replace a native speaker or a good teacher, but it's an excellent way to practise every day. This guide gives you five concrete uses and a fifteen-minute routine.

## Before you start: tell it your level and goal

The AI adjusts its vocabulary and pace if you give it context. Start each session with something like:

```prompt
I'm learning Spanish. My level is B1 (intermediate). My goal is [travel / an official exam / work / understanding TV series]. During this conversation, use vocabulary suited to my level and fairly short sentences, and correct me if I use a word wrongly.
```

Swap "Spanish" for the language you're learning. It works the same way with French, German, Italian, Portuguese and many others.

## 1. Free conversation with corrections

Talk about anything: your day, a film, your plans. The key is asking for corrections that don't interrupt the flow.

```prompt
Let's have a conversation in Spanish about [topic]. Reply naturally and end each message with a question so I keep talking. At the end of each reply, add a "Corrections" section with my mistakes, the correct version and a very short explanation in English.
```

> **Tip:** if your tool has a voice mode, use it. Speaking out loud trains pronunciation and fluency, which is exactly what's hardest to practise alone.

## 2. Real-life situations (role-play)

Rehearse specific situations before they happen: a job interview, ordering at a restaurant, a complaint at a hotel, a work call.

```prompt
Play a hotel receptionist in Madrid. I'm a guest who arrives and finds my booking isn't in the system. Speak in Spanish, naturally, and throw in a complication. When we finish, tell me which useful expressions I could have used.
```

## 3. Vocabulary in context

Memorising lists of isolated words works poorly. Learning them in sentences and situations works much better.

```prompt
Give me the 15 most useful Spanish words and expressions for [situation: going to the doctor, a work meeting, renting a flat]. For each one: the translation, an example sentence and a note on whether it's formal or informal. Put it in a table. Then give me a short quiz on them.
```

You can move the table into a flashcard app and review it over several days. To learn more about spaced repetition, see [how to study with AI](guia:study).

## 4. Correcting your writing and explaining mistakes

Write an email, an essay or a message in the language you're learning and ask for a correction that teaches you:

```prompt
Correct this text in Spanish. First give me the corrected version. Then list my mistakes grouped by type (grammar, vocabulary, word order) with a short explanation of each rule. Finally, tell me which mistake I repeat most.

[paste your text]
```

Notice the last point: discovering which mistake you repeat most tells you exactly what to study.

## 5. Preparing for official exams

If you're preparing for an official exam, AI helps you practise the format:

```prompt
I'm preparing for [exam name and level]. Give me a writing task in the format and length of that exam. When I hand it in, assess it against the usual marking criteria (task achievement, coherence, grammar, vocabulary), with an approximate score and specific advice to improve.
```

>! **Careful:** the score the AI gives you is only a rough guide. To know your real level, take official practice exams and, if you can, ask a teacher to review your work.

## A 15-minute daily routine

1. **5 minutes of conversation** about your day, with corrections at the end.
2. **5 minutes of vocabulary:** review yesterday's new words and add five more.
3. **5 minutes of writing:** write three or four sentences about something that happened to you and ask for an explained correction.

Fifteen minutes a day beats two hours on a Saturday. Consistency matters more than anything when learning a language.

## Tricks to get more out of it

- **Ask it to speak more slowly or simply** when you don't understand, instead of switching to English.
- **Ask for variants:** Spanish from Spain or Mexico, British or American English, French from France or Canada.
- **Combine it with real content:** after watching an episode of a series, ask the AI to quiz you on what happened.
- **Keep a note of your frequent mistakes** and review it every week.

## What AI doesn't replace

AI is an excellent practice partner, but it has limits. Its voice mode is good, but it won't always catch all your pronunciation mistakes. Nor does it replace talking to real people, with their accents, interruptions and local expressions. Use it to practise daily and to arrive at real conversations with more confidence.

## FAQ

### Which languages can I practise with AI?

The main assistants work very well with widely spoken languages (Spanish, English, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Chinese, Japanese and many more). Quality may be somewhat lower for languages with fewer speakers.

### Can I learn a language from scratch with AI alone?

You can make good progress, especially in vocabulary and comprehension, but it's worth combining it with a structured course or textbook so you follow a clear grammar progression.

### Is it better to practise by writing or speaking?

Both. Writing gives you time to think and consolidate grammar; speaking trains fluency. If you can, alternate both in your routine.
