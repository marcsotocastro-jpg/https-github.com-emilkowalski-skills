You don't need to spend money to make the most of artificial intelligence in your studies. Almost every major tool has a free plan that's enough for a student. The hard part isn't finding them but knowing which one to use for what. In this guide we organise them by task, with what each does well and its limits.

>! **Careful:** free plans change often: message limits, included features or available models. Always check the current terms on each tool's official website.

## For understanding topics and answering questions: chat assistants

Chat assistants are a student's Swiss army knife. They explain concepts, answer questions, generate practice questions and correct essays.

- **ChatGPT** (OpenAI): very versatile, good at explaining and with many features on its free plan.
- **Claude** (Anthropic): especially good with long texts and clear, well-structured explanations.
- **Gemini** (Google): built into Google's tools, handy if you already use Gmail, Docs or Drive.

**What to use them for:** explanations at your level, solving problems step by step, generating exam questions from your notes, correcting texts and practising languages.

**Their limit:** they can be wrong or make up facts. For assignments that need sources, use the tools in the next section. The full method is in [how to study with AI without cheating](guia:study).

```prompt
Organise these notes into an outline with headings and subheadings. Under each subheading, put the key ideas in short sentences and one example. At the end, flag the 3 concepts most likely to come up in the exam.

[paste your notes]
```

## For research with sources: AI search engines

When you write an assignment, you need to know where each fact comes from. AI search engines answer and also link to their sources.

- **Perplexity:** answers questions while citing the pages it consulted, so you can open and check them.
- **Assistants with web search turned on:** several chat assistants can search the internet and show links.

**How to use them well:** always open the cited sources and check they say what the AI claims. For academic work, prioritise reliable sources: scientific papers, official bodies, universities and reputable media.

```prompt
Find information on [topic] for an assignment for [course]. Give me a 200-word summary and a list of 5 reliable sources (official bodies, universities or academic papers) with their links and one line on what each contributes.
```

## For finding academic papers

If you're at university, there are search engines built for scientific literature. **Google Scholar** isn't a conversational AI, but it's free and essential for finding papers and seeing who cites them. Some AI academic search tools summarise papers and answer questions about them; check what their free plan includes.

> **Tip:** ask your university library for access to the databases it subscribes to. Many include paid papers you can read for free as a student.

## For presentations and visual work

- **Canva:** design for presentations, posters and infographics with templates and AI features. It has a generous free plan and, in many cases, special terms for schools.
- **Chat assistants** help you build the structure and text of each slide before you design it.

```prompt
Prepare the structure of an 8-slide presentation on [topic] for class. For each slide: a title, 3 ideas in short sentences and a suggested image or chart. The presentation should last 10 minutes.
```

## For getting organised

- **Calendars and to-do apps** with AI features, or simply a chat assistant to create your study plan.
- **Flashcard apps with spaced repetition** for memorising vocabulary, dates and formulas. You can generate the cards with an assistant and import them into the app.

## When is it worth paying?

For most students, free plans are enough. Paying may make sense if:

- You use AI every day and keep hitting the message limit.
- You need to analyse very long documents, such as a thesis or many papers at once.
- You need specific features that are only on the paid plan.

Before paying, check whether your school provides access to any tool, look for student discounts and test whether a free alternative covers what you need.

## Basic rules for using them well

1. Check your school's rules on using AI in assignments and exams.
2. Don't hand in AI-generated text as your own.
3. Check every fact and quote against the original source.
4. Don't upload classmates' work or personal data.

## FAQ

### What's the best free AI for studying?

There's no single answer. For explanations and revision, any of the main chat assistants works very well. For assignments with sources, an AI search engine that cites links. Ideally, use two or three depending on the task.

### Can teachers tell if I use AI?

There are tools that try to detect it, although they're not infallible. Beyond detection, what matters is using it to learn and following your school's rules.

### Do these tools work in other languages?

Yes. All the tools in this guide work in many languages: you can ask in your language and get answers in it.
