Artificial intelligence is an excellent helper for a CV (or resume, as it's called in the US), as long as you use it to polish and organise, not to invent. A good AI-assisted CV gives itself away by the opposite of what you'd expect: it doesn't sound like AI. It sounds like you, with clear sentences, concrete achievements and the words the employer is looking for. This guide walks you through the whole process in six steps, with a prompt for each one.

## Step 1: gather your information before opening the AI

The AI doesn't know your career. If you don't give it facts, it fills the space with generic lines like "proactive team player with strong communication skills". Before you start, write down in a document:

- Every job you've had: company, title, dates and your main tasks.
- **Achievements with numbers** wherever possible: "cut customer response time from 48 to 24 hours", "managed a budget of €20,000", "served around 60 customers a day".
- Education, courses, languages with your real level and the tools you know well.
- What sets you apart: volunteering, side projects, awards.

Don't worry about the wording: a messy list is fine. That material is what the AI will turn into good copy.

>! **Careful:** don't paste your ID number, full address or phone number into the chat. It doesn't need them to help you. Add them yourself at the end.

## Step 2: ask for a structure, not a finished text

Start with the structure. That way you choose the order and focus before the AI writes anything.

```prompt
Act as a recruiter with experience in [your industry]. Here is my messy career information. Suggest the best CV structure for a [target role] position: which sections to include, in what order and what to highlight in each. Don't write it yet.

[paste your information]
```

## Step 3: turn tasks into achievements

This is where AI helps most. Most CVs describe duties ("customer service", "order management"). The ones that get interviews describe results.

```prompt
Rewrite these duties from my experience as CV achievements. Start each bullet with a past-tense action verb, include the numbers I give you and don't invent anything that isn't there. If a bullet would be stronger with a number I don't have, mark it [ADD NUMBER] so I can look it up.

[paste the duties from one job]
```

Notice the last instruction: it stops the AI from inventing and shows you where it's worth finding a real figure.

## Step 4: write a short profile that sounds like you

The profile is the three or four lines at the top of your CV. It's the first thing people read and the place where templates show the most.

```prompt
Write three different versions of a professional profile of no more than 50 words for my CV, based on my experience. Avoid clichés like "proactive", "results-driven" or "passionate". Mention my specialism, years of experience and one concrete achievement. Professional but natural tone.
```

Pick the version that represents you best and adjust it in your own words.

## Step 5: tailor your CV to each job

Sending the same CV to every job is one of the most common reasons people don't hear back. Many companies use applicant tracking systems that filter applications by keywords from the job ad. With AI, tailoring your CV takes five minutes.

```prompt
Here are a job ad and my CV. Do three things:
1. List the keywords and requirements from the ad that don't appear in my CV.
2. Say which of those requirements I actually meet based on my experience, even if I haven't phrased it that way.
3. Suggest specific changes to my CV to reflect this, without inventing experience I don't have.

JOB AD:
[paste the ad]

MY CV:
[paste your CV without personal details]
```

> **Tip:** keep a "master" version of your CV with everything in it, and create tailored copies for each application. Put the company and date in the file name so you don't mix them up.

## Step 6: final review

Before sending, ask for a critical review:

```prompt
Review my CV as a recruiter who has 30 seconds to decide. Tell me: what grabs attention first, what's unnecessary, what's missing and any spelling, formatting or date inconsistencies you notice. Be direct.
```

Then read it aloud yourself. If there's a sentence you'd never say in an interview, change it.

## Common mistakes when writing a CV with AI

- **Inventing or exaggerating.** If an interviewer asks about something you didn't do, it shows. AI should improve the form, not the substance.
- **Copying without checking.** AI sometimes changes dates or company names, or merges jobs. Check every detail.
- **Text that's too long.** For most profiles, one or two pages is right. Explicitly ask it to cut.
- **The same tone for everything.** A CV for a tech start-up and one for a government role don't sound the same. Tell the AI what kind of employer it is.

## FAQ

### Can employers tell if a CV was written with AI?

What stands out is a generic CV, full of empty phrases with no concrete facts. If you use AI to organise and improve your real information and review it in your own words, the result is simply a good CV.

### Which tool is best for writing a CV?

Any of the main assistants will do. What matters is the information you give it and the prompts you use. If you want to compare, try the same prompt in two tools and keep the result you like best.

### Can AI design the CV?

Text assistants help with the content. For design, use word processor templates or design tools. Favour a clean format that applicant tracking systems can read easily: avoid complex tables, multiple columns and text inside images.
