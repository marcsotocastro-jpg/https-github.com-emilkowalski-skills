A cover letter has one job: explaining why you fit this role at this company, and not just any role anywhere. That's why generic letters don't work, and why asking an AI to "write me a cover letter" produces something nobody reads to the end. With the method in this guide you'll have a tailored letter in under fifteen minutes, written from your real experience and the specific job ad.

## What makes a good cover letter

Before using AI, it helps to know what we're aiming for. A good letter:

- **Is short.** Between 150 and 250 words. Whoever reads it has many more.
- **Talks about the company.** It shows you know what they do and why you're interested.
- **Connects two or three of your achievements to what the job asks for.** It doesn't repeat your CV: it interprets it.
- **Ends with a clear next step**, such as an invitation to talk.
- **Sounds like a person**, not a form.

## Step 1: gather three ingredients

The AI can only tailor the letter if you give it material. Prepare:

1. **The full job ad**, copied as it is.
2. **Your CV or main achievements**, without personal details.
3. **What you know about the company:** what it does, something recent that caught your eye (a project, a product, the way they work) and why you're genuinely interested.

The third point matters most and is the one most people skip. Spend five minutes on their website and social media. One concrete sentence about the company is worth more than three paragraphs of qualities.

## Step 2: let the AI find the connections

Before writing, ask for an analysis. That way you choose which arguments to use.

```prompt
Act as a recruiter. Here are a job ad and my experience. Identify the 3 most important requirements in the ad and, for each one, the achievement or experience of mine that best proves it. If there's a requirement I don't meet, tell me and suggest an honest way to address it.

JOB AD:
[paste the ad]

MY EXPERIENCE:
[paste your achievements or CV]
```

## Step 3: write the draft with clear instructions

Now ask for the letter with all the form and tone instructions:

```prompt
Using that analysis, write a cover letter for the [role] position at [company].
- Maximum 220 words, in three or four short paragraphs.
- First paragraph: why I'm interested in this company specifically. Use this detail: [what you know about the company].
- Second and third paragraphs: connect my two most relevant achievements to what they need.
- Closing: suggest a conversation, without pleading.
- Professional, warm tone. Avoid clichés like "I am writing to express my interest", "passionate" or "synergies".
- Don't invent anything I haven't told you.
```

## Step 4: make it sound like you

The draft is already good, but it isn't yours yet. Read it aloud and change any sentence you wouldn't say in conversation. Then ask for one last review:

```prompt
Review this letter as if you were the hiring manager. Which sentence convinces you most? Which would you cut? Does anything sound generic or exaggerated? Suggest a final version with the minimum changes needed.

[paste your letter]
```

> **Tip:** if the company has a very informal tone (a start-up, a creative agency), tell the AI and ask for a more direct style. For a public body or a law firm, ask for a more formal tone. Matching the tone matters as much as matching the content.

## Example opening paragraph: before and after

**Generic:** "I am writing to express my interest in the administrative assistant position advertised, as I believe my profile matches your requirements."

**Tailored:** "I've been following how you opened three new shops this year while keeping the personal service you're known for. I'd love to help your administration grow at the same pace, and my experience organising invoicing for a fast-growing company could be useful for exactly that."

The difference isn't fancy words. It's the specific detail about the company and the achievement that connects to it.

## When you don't need a full letter

Not every application asks for one. If the form has an optional "message" field, use it for a three- or four-sentence version with the essentials. You can ask the AI for it like this:

```prompt
Shorten this letter into a message of no more than 60 words for the comments field of an application form. Keep the detail about the company and the main achievement.
```

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Sending the same letter to several companies** and only changing the name. It shows, and sometimes people forget to change the name.
- **Repeating your CV in prose.** The letter should interpret your experience, not list it.
- **Exaggerating.** If the AI adds qualities or achievements that aren't yours, remove them.
- **Not checking the company name and job title.** It's the most common mistake and the most damaging.

## FAQ

### How long should a cover letter be?

Between 150 and 250 words is usually ideal. If the job ad asks for something specific (for example, answering a question), adjust the length accordingly.

### Can people tell a cover letter was written with AI?

It shows when the letter is generic: clichés, qualities without proof and nothing about the company. If you include real facts about the company and yourself and adjust it in your own words, it's indistinguishable from a well-written letter.

### Should the letter be in the language of the job ad?

Yes. If the ad is in English, write the letter in English. You can ask the AI to write it directly in that language and then check the level matches what you claim on your CV.
