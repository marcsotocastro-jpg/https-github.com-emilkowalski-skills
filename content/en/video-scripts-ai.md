Short videos are the format that gets the most reach on social media today, but also the hardest to prepare. Many people film without a script and end up with long videos with no rhythm that viewers abandon in the first few seconds. A simple script changes everything, and artificial intelligence is perfect for writing it. In this guide you'll see the structure that works and how to ask for each part.

## The structure of a short video

Almost every short video that works follows three parts:

1. **Hook (0-3 seconds):** a line or image that makes people stop scrolling. It's the most important part.
2. **Content (3-25 seconds):** what you promised in the hook, fast and to the point. One idea per shot.
3. **Close (final seconds):** a clear conclusion and a call to action: follow, save the video, book, comment.

For most topics, 20 to 45 seconds is a good length. If you have nothing more to say, stop.

## Step 1: ideas your audience wants to see

Start with your customers' real questions. They're the best source of ideas.

```prompt
I run [business or profile] and my audience is [who]. Give me 15 short video ideas based on real questions my audience asks. Group them into: useful tips, common mistakes, behind the scenes and before and after. For each idea, a one-line hook.
```

## Step 2: the full script

Ask for the script with timings, spoken lines and what's on screen. Filming becomes much easier.

```prompt
Write a script for a 30-second vertical video about [topic] for [platform]. My audience: [who]. My tone: [friendly, funny, expert].
Table format with columns: time, what I say, what's shown, on-screen text.
- Hook in the first 3 seconds.
- One idea per shot, sentences of no more than 12 words.
- Close with this call to action: [action].
```

> **Tip:** read the script aloud with a stopwatch before filming. If you run over, cut lines rather than speaking faster.

## Hooks: formulas that work

If the hook fails, nobody sees the rest. Ask for several options and test them:

```prompt
Give me 10 different hooks for a short video about [topic], using these formulas: direct question, common mistake ("If you do this, you're…"), surprising result, specific promise ("3 tricks to…"), before/after contrast and a bold but honest opinion. No more than 10 words each.
```

Some examples for a hair salon:

- "Your shampoo might be why your hair frizzes."
- "Three things your hairdresser notices the moment you sit down."
- "The same haircut, dried properly and not."

>! **Careful:** the hook has to deliver on its promise. Exaggerated hooks that don't pay off make people leave the video and distrust your account.

## Step 3: on-screen text and captions

Many people watch without sound. Captions and on-screen text are essential. Many editing apps generate automatic captions from the audio; always check them, because they get names and technical words wrong.

```prompt
From the previous script, extract the on-screen text: phrases of no more than 6 words for each shot, understandable without audio.
```

## Step 4: description, title and hashtags

```prompt
Write the description for posting this video on [platform]: an opening line that complements the video (rather than repeating it), a question to encourage comments and 4 hashtags specific to my industry and area.
```

For YouTube Shorts, also ask for a clear title under 60 characters that includes the words people would search for.

## Formats that perform well

- **Quick list:** "3 mistakes when…", "5 things that…". Easy to film and understand.
- **Process:** how something is made, start to finish, sped up.
- **Before and after:** very visual, ideal for services.
- **Reply to a comment:** use a real follower's question as the hook.
- **Myth or fact:** debunk a common belief in your industry.

```prompt
Turn this topic into 3 twenty-second scripts in different formats (quick list, myth or fact, and reply to a comment): [topic].
```

## Reuse every script

A good script can become several pieces:

- An Instagram **carousel** with the same ideas (see how to ask for one in [how to create Instagram content with AI](guia:instagram)).
- A **text post** for LinkedIn or Facebook.
- A **short article** for your website or blog.

```prompt
Turn this video script into a 6-slide carousel and a 120-word text post for LinkedIn.

[paste the script]
```

## Common mistakes

- **Starting by introducing yourself.** "Hi, I'm Martha and today I'm going to talk about…" makes many people leave. Start with the hook.
- **Packing in too many ideas.** One video, one main idea.
- **Reading the script robotically.** The script is a guide. Learn the ideas and say them in your own words.
- **Not checking automatic captions.**

## FAQ

### What's the best length for a short video?

There's no magic number. What matters is that the video lasts as long as the content needs and that people watch to the end. For quick tips, 20 to 40 seconds usually works well.

### Can AI create the whole video?

There are tools that generate videos from text, but for a business or personal brand, videos you film yourself build more trust. Use AI for the script, ideas and editing.

### How many videos should I post?

As many as you can keep up. Two or three a week for several months usually gets better results than posting a lot at first and then giving up.
