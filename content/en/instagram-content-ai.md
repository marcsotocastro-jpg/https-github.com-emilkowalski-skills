Posting consistently on Instagram is exhausting when you have to think every day about what to share and how to say it. Artificial intelligence can't take photos of your product or film your day, but it can help with what blocks you most: ideas, organisation and captions. In this guide you'll see how to build a monthly content system in one afternoon while keeping your own voice.

## Step 1: describe your account in one paragraph

This step is what separates generic content from content that feels like yours. Write a description you'll use at the start of every prompt:

```prompt
Context for my Instagram account:
- Who I am: [business or profile, what you do].
- Who I'm talking to: [your ideal customer, age, interests, what problem they have].
- What I want to achieve: [sales, bookings, shop visits, community].
- My tone: [friendly, funny, expert, inspiring]. Words I use: [examples]. Words I'd never use: [examples].
- What makes me different: [what competitors don't have].
```

Save it in your notes and paste it every time. The more specific it is, the better the results.

## Step 2: content pillars

Pillars are the three or four categories of topics you post about. They give you variety without improvising.

```prompt
[paste your context]
Suggest 4 content pillars for my account, with one sentence explaining the goal of each and 5 post ideas per pillar.
```

An example for a café: product (what we serve and how it's made), behind the counter (the team and daily life), neighbourhood (local plans and shops) and community (customers, questions, celebrations).

## Step 3: the monthly calendar

With your pillars, ask for a realistic calendar. Three posts a week you actually publish beat a daily plan you abandon.

```prompt
[paste your context]
Create a content calendar for [month] with 3 posts per week, rotating my pillars. For each post: day, pillar, format (reel, carousel, photo or story), the idea in one sentence and what I need to film or photograph. Take these dates into account: [holidays, campaigns, local events]. Present it as a table.
```

> **Tip:** set aside one afternoon a month to film and photograph all the material in the calendar at once. It saves a huge amount of time compared with preparing each post on the day.

## Step 4: captions that don't sound like AI

Careless AI captions are easy to spot: too many emojis, empty inspirational lines, endless hashtags. Ask for exactly the opposite:

```prompt
[paste your context]
Write the caption for this post: [idea]. Requirements:
- An opening line that hooks and makes sense on its own (it's the only part visible without tapping).
- Maximum 100 words.
- Write the way I'd talk, in short sentences.
- Maximum 2 emojis.
- End with a question or a clear call to action.
- 5 relevant, specific hashtags, not generic ones.
Give me 3 versions with different opening lines.
```

## Formats: how to ask for each one

### Reels

For short videos, the first seconds matter most. There's a complete guide in [how to write short video scripts with AI](guia:video-scripts).

```prompt
[paste your context]
Give me 10 reel ideas for my account. For each one: the hook for the first 3 seconds (on-screen text or a line), what's shown in the video and the recommended length.
```

### Carousels

Carousels work very well for useful content people save.

```prompt
[paste your context]
Create a 7-slide carousel about [a topic useful to my customer]. Slide 1: a curiosity-sparking headline (no more than 8 words). Slides 2 to 6: one idea per slide, no more than 20 words each. Slide 7: a summary and call to action. Also suggest the image or design to go with each.
```

### Stories

```prompt
[paste your context]
Suggest a sequence of 5 stories to [goal: announce a new product, get weekend bookings]. Include a poll or interactive question and finish with a link or call to action.
```

## Replying to comments and messages

Replying to comments helps build community. For frequent comments, prepare base replies with AI and personalise them. For criticism, use the same method we explain in [how to reply to reviews with AI](guia:reviews).

## Images

For your product and business, real photos always build more trust than generated images. AI is useful for:

- Composition and lighting ideas for your photos.
- Backgrounds, templates or supporting illustrations for carousels.
- Editing and enhancing photos with the AI features in design apps.

If you want to generate images, read [how to write prompts to create images with AI](guia:images).

>! **Careful:** don't present AI-generated images as real photos of your product or premises. Besides misleading customers, it causes problems when the product they receive doesn't look like what they saw.

## Analyse what works

Once a month, note the basic stats for your posts (reach, saves, comments, profile visits) and ask for an analysis:

```prompt
Here are the stats for my posts from last month: [table with post, format, reach, saves, comments]. What type of content works best? What should I post more and less of next month? Give me 3 specific conclusions.
```

## FAQ

### Does the algorithm penalise AI-made content?

What matters is whether your audience finds the content interesting: whether they watch it, save it and comment. Generic content performs worse whether a person or an AI wrote it. Use AI to help and add the personal part yourself.

### How many times a week should I post?

It depends on how much time you have. Three quality posts a week, kept up for months, usually work better than posting daily for two weeks and disappearing.

### Which tool is best for this?

Any major chat assistant works for ideas and captions. For designing carousels and covers, a design tool with templates will save you a lot of time.
