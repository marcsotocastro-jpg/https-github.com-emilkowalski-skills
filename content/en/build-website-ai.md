A few years ago, building a website meant knowing how to code or paying someone. Today there are tools that generate a site from a description and assistants that write the copy for you. Even so, a website that works doesn't come from a single click: it needs a clear goal, good copy and a careful review. This guide covers the whole process in six steps, designed for small businesses, freelancers and personal projects.

## Step 1: define what the website is for

Before opening any tool, answer three questions. They may seem obvious, but they completely change the result:

1. **Who will visit it?** Local customers, businesses, people searching on Google from their phone…
2. **What do you want them to do?** Call, book, buy, message you on WhatsApp, request a quote.
3. **What do they need to know to do it?** Prices, opening hours, location, examples of your work, reviews.

```prompt
I run a [type of business] in [city]. My customers are [type of customers]. I want my website to get them to [main action]. Help me define: which pages I need, what must appear on the home page at first glance and which information is essential for a customer to decide.
```

## Step 2: create the structure

A small website usually needs few pages: home, services or products, about, contact and the legal pages. Ask the AI for a detailed structure:

```prompt
Create the structure of a website for [business]. For each page, list the sections in order and what each should contain. Include where to place the main button ([action]) so it's always visible on mobile.
```

## Step 3: write the copy

This is where AI saves hours. The key is giving it your real details and asking for short copy, because people skim online, mostly on their phones.

```prompt
Write the home page copy for my website. Details: [name], [what you offer], [what makes you different], [area], [approximate price if you want to show it]. Include: a headline of no more than 8 words that clearly says what I do, a supporting sentence, 3 benefits in short sentences and the text for the main button. [Friendly/professional] tone. No clichés like "end-to-end solutions" or "quality and professionalism".
```

> **Tip:** write the way your customers talk. If they search for "emergency plumber in Leeds", use those words in your headline rather than "comprehensive plumbing services". It helps people find you on Google and understand you instantly.

## Step 4: choose how to build it

There are three routes, from simplest to most flexible:

| Option | Who it's for | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Website builders with templates and AI | Businesses that want something fast | Easy, hosting included, no coding | Monthly fee, less design freedom |
| AI site generators from a description | Prototypes and simple projects | Very fast, you can ask for changes in plain words | Review the result carefully and check where it's hosted |
| Custom site with help from a coding assistant | People who want full control or have some technical background | Total freedom, low cost | Takes more time and review |

For most small businesses, a website builder with templates is the safest option. Choose one that lets you use your own domain, looks good on mobile and lets you edit text easily.

## Step 5: check what really matters

Before publishing, go through this list. AI can help with each point, but do the final check yourself, on your phone:

- **Mobile:** can you read it without zooming? Is the main button visible without scrolling?
- **Speed:** does it load quickly on mobile data? Very heavy images are the most common cause of slowness.
- **Contact details:** phone, address, opening hours. Check every number: a mistake here costs customers.
- **Copy:** read it aloud. Fix any details the AI invented (prices, years of experience, awards).
- **Legal pages:** legal notice, privacy policy and cookie policy. In the EU and many other places they're required if you run a business or collect data.
- **Google:** register your site in Google Search Console and create or update your Google Business Profile.

>! **Careful:** legal texts must match your actual situation. You can use AI to understand what they should include, but have the result reviewed by a professional or use a specialist service, especially if you sell online.

## Step 6: get found

A published website doesn't get visitors on its own. To start:

1. Create or complete your **Google Business Profile** with photos, opening hours and a link to your site.
2. Ask happy customers to leave a **review** (and reply to them, as explained in [how to reply to reviews with AI](guia:reviews)).
3. Add the link to your **social media** and your email signature.
4. Occasionally publish content that's useful to your customers, such as FAQs or tips.

```prompt
Give me 10 ideas for short blog posts for my [business] that answer questions my customers search for on Google. For each idea, the title and the 3 questions it should answer.
```

## Common mistakes

- **Publishing AI copy without reviewing it**, with invented details or generic phrases.
- **Too many pages and too little content.** Five complete pages are better than fifteen empty ones.
- **Not showing prices, not even approximate ones**, when your customers look for them.
- **Forgetting mobile.** Most visits to local businesses come from phones.

## FAQ

### Can I build a website for free with AI?

Many tools have a free plan to try them out, but for a business it's worth paying for a plan that lets you use your own domain and removes the platform's ads. Add the cost of the domain too, which is usually a small yearly fee.

### How long does it take?

With copy prepared with AI and a template, a simple website can be ready in a day or two. What usually takes longest is gathering real photos, prices and details.

### Can AI handle my Google ranking?

It helps you write clear copy, titles and descriptions, and generate content ideas. But ranking also depends on your business profile, reviews, site speed and publishing consistently.
