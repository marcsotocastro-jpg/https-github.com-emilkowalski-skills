Tools that create images from text can produce illustrations, backgrounds, sketches or supporting images in seconds. But if you type "a cat", you get any old cat. To get what's in your head you need to describe it like a director of photography would. In this guide you'll learn a simple formula, see examples and find out what you can and can't do with generated images.

## Where to create images with AI

There are several ways to do it:

- **Chat assistants with image generation:** some general assistants let you ask for images in the same conversation.
- **Design tools with AI:** design apps include features to generate images, remove backgrounds or upscale photos.
- **Specialised image tools**, which usually offer more control over style.

Terms of use, free limits and quality change often. Try two or three and stick with the one that best fits what you need.

## The formula: six parts

A good image prompt describes:

1. **Subject:** what appears, in detail. Not "a woman" but "a woman in her sixties wearing an apron, kneading bread".
2. **Action and setting:** what's happening and where. "In a rustic kitchen with flour on the table."
3. **Style:** photography, flat illustration, watercolour, 3D, pencil drawing, vintage poster.
4. **Light and colour:** soft morning light, warm sunset, studio with a white background, pastel colours, high contrast.
5. **Framing:** close-up, wide shot, top-down view, side profile, blurred background.
6. **Format:** square for a post, vertical for stories, horizontal for a website header.

## Examples: before and after

**Before:** "A cup of coffee."

**After:**

```prompt
Product photograph of a sand-coloured handmade ceramic mug with steaming coffee, on a light wooden table. Soft natural morning light coming from the left. Close-up with a blurred background where a window with plants can be made out. Warm, natural tones. Square format.
```

**Before:** "Illustration of a person working."

**After:**

```prompt
Flat editorial-style illustration: a young person working on a laptop at a desk by a window, with a mug and a plant. Limited palette of navy blue, mustard yellow and off-white. Simple shapes, no text. Horizontal 16:9 format for a blog header.
```

> **Tip:** if you're not sure how to describe a style, ask a text assistant: "describe in detail the visual style of a 1950s travel poster so I can use it in an image prompt".

## Refining the result

The first image is rarely the final one. Work in iterations:

- **Change one thing at a time.** If you change style, light and framing all at once, you won't know what improved the result.
- **Say what you don't want.** Many tools accept instructions like "no text", "no people" or "plain background".
- **Ask for variations** of the image you like most.
- **Use a reference image** if the tool allows it, as long as you have the right to use it.

## Common problems and how to avoid them

- **Text inside the image with garbled letters.** Tools have improved but still slip up. It's better to generate the image without text and add it later in a design tool.
- **Odd hands, fingers and details.** Zoom in and check the image before using it.
- **Everything looks too perfect.** Ask for "natural look", "documentary photography" or "real imperfections" if you want realism.
- **Inconsistent style** across several images. Save the prompt that worked and reuse it, changing only the subject.

## What you can and can't do with the images

Before using a generated image, especially commercially:

1. **Read the tool's terms.** They state whether you can use images commercially and with what limits.
2. **Don't imitate real people** without permission, or create images that could pass as real photos of someone.
3. **Don't copy protected brands or characters** (logos, film characters, specific works by an artist).
4. **Don't mislead:** don't use a generated image as if it were a real photo of your product, premises or team.

>! **Careful:** copyright rules for AI-generated images are still evolving and vary by country. For important commercial uses, such as a logo or a campaign, consult a professional.

## Where image AI shines

- **Illustrations for articles or presentations** when no real photo is possible.
- **Sketches and moodboards** to explain an idea to a designer or client.
- **Backgrounds and textures** for social media designs.
- **Composition ideas** before taking your own product photos.

## A template to keep

```prompt
[Style: photograph / illustration / watercolour…] of [subject in detail] [action] in [setting]. [Type of] light. [Palette] colours. [Shot and angle] framing. No [what you don't want]. [Square / vertical / horizontal] format.
```

## FAQ

### Can I use AI-generated images on my website or social media?

Generally yes, if the tool's terms allow it. Check them, especially for commercial use, and avoid images that imitate real people, brands or protected characters.

### Why do images come out with misspelled text?

Generating text inside an image is still hard for many tools. The most reliable fix is to generate the image without text and add it in a design app.

### Should I write the prompt in English?

Many tools understand other languages well, but some respond slightly better to English. If the result isn't right, try the same prompt in English.
