The best way to prepare for an interview is to practise out loud. The problem is you don't always have someone willing to play the interviewer. An AI can fill that role very well: it asks questions tailored to the job, listens to your answers and tells you what to improve, as many times as you like. In this guide you'll see how to prepare in five steps.

## Step 1: research the role and the company

Start by understanding what they're looking for. Paste the job ad and ask for an analysis:

```prompt
Analyse this job ad. Tell me: the 5 most important skills they'll assess, the kind of questions usually asked to test each one, and any red flags they might look for in a candidate. Role: [role]. Company: [type of company and industry].

[paste the job ad]
```

Add your own research: visit the company's website, social media and any recent news. AI doesn't always have up-to-date information about specific companies, especially small ones, so don't ask it for company facts: give them to it.

## Step 2: prepare your stories with the STAR method

Many interview questions ask for examples: "tell me about a time you resolved a conflict", "talk about a mistake you made". The clearest way to answer is the STAR method:

- **Situation:** the context, in one sentence.
- **Task:** what you were responsible for.
- **Action:** what you did, specifically.
- **Result:** what happened, with a number if possible, and what you learned.

Prepare four or five stories from your experience and ask for help structuring them:

```prompt
I'll tell you about a situation at work. Help me turn it into an interview answer using the STAR method, no longer than 90 seconds when spoken. Don't invent anything: if any part is missing information, ask me.

[describe the situation in your own words]
```

With four good stories you can answer most behavioural questions, because one story can work for several questions.

## Step 3: run a mock interview

This is the most useful step. Ask the AI to interview you properly, one question at a time:

```prompt
Act as a hiring manager interviewing me for a [role] position at a [industry] company. Run an 8-question interview mixing questions about my experience, behavioural questions and at least one tough one. Rules:
- Ask ONE question at a time and wait for my answer.
- After each answer, give brief feedback: what worked, what to improve and a stronger version of my answer.
- At the end, give me an overall assessment and the 3 things I most need to work on.
```

> **Tip:** if your tool has a voice mode, use it. Answering out loud is much closer to a real interview than typing, and it helps you notice filler words and answers that run too long.

## Step 4: prepare for the tough questions

Some questions make almost everyone nervous. Practise them specifically:

- "Why do you want to leave your current job?"
- "What's your biggest weakness?"
- "Why is there a gap in your CV?"
- "What salary are you expecting?"
- "Where do you see yourself in five years?"

```prompt
I'm going to answer this tough interview question: "[question]". My real situation is: [explain honestly]. Help me prepare an answer that's honest, short and positive, without lying or sounding rehearsed. Give me two versions.
```

For the salary question, research typical ranges for your role and location on job sites and salary surveys first. Don't trust figures from an AI without a source.

## Step 5: prepare your own questions

You'll almost always be asked "do you have any questions?". Saying no is a missed opportunity.

```prompt
Suggest 8 smart questions I can ask at the end of an interview for a [role] position. They should show genuine interest and help me decide whether the job is right for me. Avoid questions about holidays or salary in a first interview.
```

Pick two or three that you genuinely care about.

## The day before: quick review

- Reread the job ad and your notes on the company.
- Go over your four or five STAR stories.
- Do one last short mock interview with five questions.
- Prepare your clothes, your route or your connection if it's online.
- Get a good night's sleep. No AI replaces that.

>! **Careful:** don't use AI during a live interview to feed you answers. Besides being easy to spot, it can disqualify you. Use it to prepare beforehand, not to answer for you.

## FAQ

### Does the AI know which questions each company asks?

Not reliably. It can give you the typical questions for a role and industry, which tend to be very close to the real ones. For a specific company's questions, look for other candidates' experiences on job sites.

### How many mock interviews should I do?

Two or three full mock interviews are usually enough to make a big difference in confidence and clarity. What matters is reviewing the feedback and improving between sessions.

### Does it work for interviews in another language?

Yes, and it's one of its best uses. Ask for the interview in that language and, alongside feedback on content, ask it to correct grammar and vocabulary mistakes.
