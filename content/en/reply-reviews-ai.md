Google reviews are the shop window of any local business. Before choosing a restaurant, a hairdresser or a plumber, many people read what other customers say, and how the business replies. Replying well shows care and professionalism. The problem is that it takes time, and with negative reviews, it's hard to find the right tone. AI helps with both.

## Why replying is worth it

- **Future customers read your replies.** A good reply to criticism can be more convincing than ten five-star reviews.
- **It shows you listen.** Someone who leaves a negative review and gets a sincere reply sometimes changes their mind.
- **It helps you improve.** Reviews are free information about what works and what doesn't.

## Write your "tone sheet" once

To make every reply sound like your business and not like a machine, write a description you'll paste at the start of each request:

```prompt
Context for replying to reviews for my business:
- Business: [name and type], in [city/area].
- Who replies: [your name and role, for example "Martha, the owner"].
- Tone: warm and natural, first names / formal.
- What makes us different: [for example, local produce, family service].
- Rules: replies of no more than 80 words, no template phrases like "thank you for your feedback", no excess emojis, never argue with the customer.
```

Save it in your notes. Every time you reply, paste it and add the review.

## Positive reviews: make them personal

The classic mistake is replying "Thanks for your review!" to every one. A good reply mentions something specific the customer said.

```prompt
[paste your tone sheet]
Reply to this 5-star review. Mention something specific from what they say, thank them without overdoing it and invite them back with a natural detail (for example, a new dish or season). Maximum 50 words.

Review: "[paste the review]"
```

**Example.** Review: "Everything was delicious, especially the croquettes. We'll be back." Reply: "Thank you, Laura! The croquettes are my mum's recipe, so we'll tell her, she'll be thrilled. In November we start our winter stews, in case you fancy trying them. Best, Martha."

## Negative reviews: the four-step method

A good reply to criticism follows this structure:

1. **Thank them and acknowledge** the problem, without getting defensive.
2. **Briefly explain** what happened, if useful, without excuses or blaming the customer.
3. **Offer a solution** or explain what you'll change.
4. **Invite them to continue privately** with a direct contact.

```prompt
[paste your tone sheet]
Reply to this negative review following these steps: thank them and acknowledge the problem, explain briefly without excuses, offer a concrete solution and give a direct contact to continue privately. Internal context (don't copy it word for word, use it to understand): [what really happened]. Maximum 90 words.

Review: "[paste the review]"
```

> **Tip:** wait until you're calm before replying to criticism. Draft the reply with AI, read it again the next day and only post it if it doesn't sound defensive.

## Unfair or fake reviews

Sometimes a review is unfair, exaggerated or from someone who was never a customer. Even so, reply calmly: other customers will read your reply, not just that person.

```prompt
[paste your tone sheet]
We believe this review isn't from a real customer (we have no record of the booking it mentions). Write a polite, short reply that says so without accusing anyone, invites them to get in touch to clear it up and comes across as professional to anyone reading. Maximum 70 words.

Review: "[paste the review]"
```

If a review breaks Google's rules (for example, it contains insults, is spam or is about another business), you can report it from your business profile. The final decision is Google's.

>! **Careful:** don't buy reviews or ask AI to write fake reviews about your business or competitors. It's against Google's rules, can get your profile removed and, depending on where you are, may be illegal.

## Analyse reviews to improve

Once a month, copy your latest reviews (without names) and ask for an analysis:

```prompt
Analyse these reviews of my business. Tell me the 3 most praised aspects, the 3 most repeated complaints and one concrete, low-cost improvement for each complaint.

[paste the reviews]
```

## Ask for more reviews (without pushing)

Happy customers rarely leave a review unless asked. Prepare a friendly message to send after a purchase or service:

```prompt
Write a short WhatsApp message asking a customer who just [service] to leave a Google review if they were happy. Natural, no pressure, with a placeholder for the link [LINK]. Maximum 40 words.
```

## FAQ

### Should I reply to every review?

Ideally, reply to every negative review and most positive ones, especially those that mention something specific. If you have many, prioritise recent ones and those with text.

### How quickly should I reply?

As soon as possible, ideally within one or two days. With a tone sheet ready and AI, each reply takes a minute or two.

### Can people tell the reply was written by AI?

It shows when every reply is identical and generic. If you personalise with details from the review, sign with your name and review the text, the reply sounds like you.
