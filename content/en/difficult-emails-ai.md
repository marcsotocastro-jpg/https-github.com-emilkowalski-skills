Some emails take a minute and others sit open all afternoon: chasing something you're owed, saying no to a client, asking for a raise or answering someone who's angry. This is where AI is especially useful, because it helps you find the right tone without letting nerves take over. Here's a method and examples for the most common cases.

## The method: facts, goal and tone

A difficult email works when the AI knows three things:

1. **The facts**, without embellishment: what happened, with dates and amounts.
2. **The goal:** exactly what you want. A refund, a meeting, an extension, closing the matter.
3. **The tone and relationship:** who you're writing to and how you want to come across. Your manager isn't a phone company.

And one instruction that makes a big difference: ask for the email to be **short**. Long difficult emails read badly and sound defensive.

## General template

```prompt
Help me write a difficult email.
- Recipient and relationship: [who they are and how we know each other].
- Facts: [what happened, with dates and amounts].
- Goal: [what I want to achieve].
- Tone: [firm, conciliatory, friendly, formal].
- Maximum [number] words. No clichés or unnecessary apologies.
Give me two versions: one softer and one more direct.
```

Asking for two versions shows you the range so you can pick the right point.

## Case 1: complaining about a charge or a service

The key is to be specific and ask for a solution with a deadline.

```prompt
Write an email complaining to my internet provider. They charged me twice for my September bill (€45.90 on the 3rd and again on the 5th). I want the duplicate charge refunded within 10 days. Firm, polite tone. Include my customer number as [CUSTOMER NUMBER]. Maximum 120 words.
```

> **Tip:** always keep a copy of the email and the reply. If the complaint goes nowhere, you'll have everything documented to escalate it to a consumer protection body or ombudsman.

## Case 2: saying no without closing the door

Saying no to a client, a colleague or a proposal is uncomfortable. A good "no" is clear, brief and, if possible, offers an alternative.

```prompt
I need to turn down a project from a regular client because I don't have time this month. I want to keep the relationship strong and suggest starting on the 15th of next month. Write a short, warm, clear email without over-explaining. Maximum 90 words.
```

## Case 3: asking for a raise

Here the email shouldn't ask for the raise directly but for a meeting, and you should come with arguments.

```prompt
I want to ask my manager for a meeting to discuss my salary. I've been at the company for two years, I've taken on coordinating two new colleagues and the project I lead has cut delivery times by 20%. Write a short email asking for the meeting: professional and confident, not demanding, and without putting all my arguments in writing. Maximum 100 words.
```

Then use AI to prepare the conversation: ask it to play your manager and practise your arguments.

## Case 4: replying to an angry customer

When someone is angry, the first step is to acknowledge it. Then explain without excuses and offer a solution.

```prompt
A customer has written to us very upset because their order arrived broken. Write a reply that:
1. Acknowledges the problem and apologises once, without overdoing it.
2. Explains the solution: we're sending a new one today at no cost and they don't need to return the broken one.
3. Gives them a direct contact in case they need anything else.
Human, warm tone. No template phrases like "we apologise for any inconvenience caused". Maximum 110 words.
```

## Case 5: chasing an unpaid invoice

```prompt
Write a friendly reminder to a client who hasn't paid a €600 invoice that was due 15 days ago. It's the second reminder. I want to stay cordial but make it clear I need payment by Friday. Include the invoice number as [INVOICE NO.]. Maximum 90 words.
```

## Before sending: the 24-hour test

If you're angry or anxious, write the email with AI, but don't send it straight away. The next day, ask for a review:

```prompt
Review this email. Does it sound aggressive, passive-aggressive or insecure anywhere? Is it clear what I'm asking for? Suggest the minimum changes to make it firm, clear and respectful.

[paste your email]
```

>! **Careful:** don't paste other people's personal data, account numbers or confidential company information. Use labels like [CLIENT] or [AMOUNT] and fill them in yourself at the end. There's more advice in [what not to share with an AI](guia:privacy).

## Common mistakes

- **Apologising too much.** One sincere apology is enough. Three in a row undermine your position.
- **Mixing topics.** One difficult email, one subject.
- **Not asking for anything specific.** If you don't say what you want and by when, the reply will be vague.
- **Sending in the heat of the moment.** AI helps you calm the tone, but deciding when to send is up to you.

## FAQ

### Can people tell an email was written with AI?

It shows when the email is long, full of polite formulas and short on facts. If you give the facts, ask for brevity and adjust it in your own words, it sounds like you.

### Can I use it for emails in another language?

Yes. You can write your instructions in your own language and ask for the email in another, specifying the level of formality. Ask it to explain any expression you don't understand before you send it.

### What about WhatsApp or Slack messages?

It works the same way. Mention the channel and ask for a shorter, more informal message. For customer service on WhatsApp, see [how to use AI with WhatsApp Business](guia:whatsapp).
