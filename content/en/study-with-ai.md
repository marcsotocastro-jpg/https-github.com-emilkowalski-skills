Artificial intelligence can be the best private tutor you've ever had or the fastest way to learn nothing at all. The difference is how you use it. If you ask it to do your homework, you'll pass the assignment and fail the exam. If you use it to understand, practise and review, you'll learn faster. This guide gives you a five-step method with prompts for each one.

## What research says about learning

Two study techniques have strong evidence behind them, and both fit perfectly with AI:

- **Active recall:** trying to remember something without looking (for example, by answering questions) fixes information better than rereading notes.
- **Spaced repetition:** reviewing a topic several times with days in between works better than cramming it all at once.

AI is ideal for both because it can generate endless questions and adapt to what you get wrong.

## Step 1: understand the topic at your level

Textbooks don't always explain things the way you need. Ask for explanations tailored to you:

```prompt
Explain [topic] as if I were 15. Use an everyday example and a comparison. Then explain it again at the level of [course or subject], using the correct technical vocabulary.
```

If something still isn't clear, keep asking: "I don't get the part about…", "give me another example", "what's the difference between X and Y?". You can ask an AI the same thing ten times.

>! **Careful:** AI can be wrong, especially with dates, very specific facts or highly specialised topics. Always cross-check with your notes and course textbook. If they disagree, go with your teacher.

## Step 2: turn your notes into questions

This is the most powerful step. Paste your notes (or part of them) and ask for questions:

```prompt
Based on these notes, give me 10 review questions: 4 short-answer, 3 multiple-choice with 4 options and 3 that make me explain in my own words. Ask ONE question at a time, wait for my answer, correct me and explain my mistake if I get it wrong. At the end, tell me which topics I should review.

[paste your notes]
```

Using your own notes as the source means the questions match what's actually on your exam.

## Step 3: explain it to the AI yourself

One of the best ways to check whether you understand something is to explain it. Flip the conversation:

```prompt
I'm going to explain [topic] in my own words. Act as a demanding teacher: tell me what I explained well, what's wrong, what I left out, and ask me a question about the weakest part.
```

If you struggle to explain it, you know exactly what to study.

## Step 4: create flashcards

Flashcards are perfect for vocabulary, dates, formulas and definitions.

```prompt
Create 20 flashcards about [topic] from my notes. Format: a table with two columns, "Question" and "Answer". Answers of no more than 15 words.
```

You can copy the table into a flashcard app with spaced repetition or into a spreadsheet, or print it and cut it out.

## Step 5: build a realistic study plan

Before exams, AI helps you spread the work:

```prompt
I have exams in [subjects] on [dates]. I can study [hours] a day on weekdays and [hours] at weekends. My weakest topics are [topics]. Make me a day-by-day study plan that includes spaced reviews of each topic and a buffer day before each exam. Present it as a table.
```

> **Tip:** be honest about how many hours you can really study. A realistic plan you stick to is worth more than a perfect one you abandon on day three.

## What you shouldn't do

- **Ask it to do the work for you.** If your school detects AI-written work, the consequences can be serious. And more importantly: you won't have learned anything.
- **Copy answers without understanding them.** If you use AI to solve a maths problem, ask it to explain the method step by step and then try a similar problem on your own.
- **Trust quotes and references.** AI sometimes invents books, authors and papers. Always find the real source.

Check your school's rules on AI. Many allow it for studying and revision but not for writing assignments. There's more general advice in [the most common mistakes when using AI](guia:mistakes).

## Example of a 45-minute study session

1. **10 minutes:** read your notes on the topic and ask the AI to clarify anything you don't understand.
2. **15 minutes:** answer 10 questions generated from your notes.
3. **10 minutes:** explain the topic in your own words and fix what it points out.
4. **10 minutes:** create or review flashcards for what you got wrong.

Repeat the question round two or three days later and again a week after that.

## FAQ

### Is using AI to study cheating?

Using it to understand, practise and review isn't cheating: it's like having a private tutor. It's cheating to hand in AI-generated work as your own when the rules forbid it.

### Can AI mark my essays?

Yes, and it's very useful if you ask it to explain each mistake instead of just returning a corrected text. That way you learn not to repeat it.

### What if the AI gives me a different answer from the textbook?

Trust the textbook and your teacher. You can ask the AI why it gave that answer, but when in doubt, your course's official source wins.
