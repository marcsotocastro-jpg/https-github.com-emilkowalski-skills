A prompt is the message you write to an artificial intelligence to ask it for something. It can be a question, an instruction or a long text full of details. The quality of the answer depends almost entirely on the quality of that message: ask for something vague and you get something vague back. In this guide you'll learn a five-part formula that works in any assistant, with examples you can copy today.

## Why the same AI gives good and bad answers

AI assistants can't read your mind. They don't know who you are, what the text is for or what you've already tried. When you write "write an email to my boss", the AI has to guess the tone, the length, the reason and even your relationship with that person. It fills the gaps with the most generic option, which is why the result sounds like a template.

The fix isn't learning strange tricks. It's giving the AI the same information you'd give a new colleague you were handing a task to: what you need, who it's for, which facts to use and how you want to receive it.

## The five-part formula

You don't always need all five parts, but the more you include, the better the result. Think of them as a checklist.

1. **Role:** who you want the AI to be. "Act as a secondary school maths teacher" or "as a customer service expert".
2. **Task:** exactly what it has to do, with a clear verb. Summarise, write, compare, fix, suggest, explain.
3. **Context:** the information only you have. Who it's for, why you need it, which facts to use, what you've already tried.
4. **Format:** how you want the answer. A table, a five-point list, an email under 120 words, a 30-second script.
5. **Tone:** how it should sound. Friendly, formal, direct, playful, suitable for a ten-year-old.

Here's a prompt with all five parts:

```prompt
Act as a customer service expert (role). Write a reply to a customer who is complaining that her order arrived 5 days late (task). We're a small online shop selling natural cosmetics; the delay was the courier's fault and we want to offer her 10% off her next order (context). Maximum 100 words, no bullet points (format). Kind and sincere, without sounding like a template (tone).
```

Compare that message with "reply to an angry customer about a delay". The first produces a reply you can send almost as is. The second, one you'll have to rewrite from scratch.

## Before and after: three real examples

### Studying

Before: "Explain the French Revolution."

After:

```prompt
Explain the causes of the French Revolution as if I were 15. Use three main causes, each with a concrete example. Finish with 3 multiple-choice questions to check I've understood, and don't give me the answers until I reply.
```

### Work

Before: "Summarise this document."

After:

```prompt
Summarise the document below for my manager, who only has two minutes. I want: one sentence with the main conclusion, three key points with figures if there are any, and a list of the decisions we need to make. If something in the document is unclear, tell me instead of guessing.

[paste the document here]
```

### Everyday life

Before: "Give me recipes."

After:

```prompt
Act as a practical home cook. Give me a Monday-to-Friday dinner plan for two people on a tight budget, with each meal ready in under 30 minutes. We don't like fish. Present it in a table with the dish, ingredients and time, and add a shopping list at the end grouped by supermarket aisle.
```

> **Tip:** when you're not sure what context to give, end your prompt with "Before answering, ask me any questions you need to do this well". The AI will ask for what's missing and the final answer will be much better.

## Have a conversation: the first answer is a draft

Many people give up after the first answer. That's a mistake. An AI assistant works best as a conversation: ask, review and refine. These follow-ups work very well:

- "Make it shorter, half the length."
- "Make it warmer, as if you were telling a friend."
- "Remove the clichés and the fancy words."
- "Give me three different versions to choose from."
- "What would make this excellent? Improve it."
- "Explain why you chose that option."

Each correction teaches the AI what you're after within that conversation. If you switch to a completely different topic, start a new chat so the earlier context doesn't get in the way.

## Mistakes that ruin a prompt

- **Asking for too many things at once.** If you need an email, a summary and a plan, ask for them in separate messages.
- **Leaving out the facts.** The AI doesn't know your opening hours, your prices or your client's name. If you don't provide them, it will make them up.
- **Asking for opinions as if they were facts.** "What's the best laptop?" depends on what you need it for. Explain your use and budget.
- **Accepting figures without checking them.** AI can invent data with complete confidence. We cover this in [the most common mistakes when using AI](guia:mistakes).

>! **Careful:** don't paste passwords, bank details, medical reports or confidential company information into a prompt. Read [what you should never share with an AI](guia:privacy) before you start.

## A template to keep

Copy this template into your notes and fill in the gaps whenever you have a new task:

```prompt
Act as [role]. I need you to [task with a clear verb].
Context: [who it's for, why I need it, key facts].
Format: [list, table, email, maximum length].
Tone: [friendly, formal, direct…].
If any information is missing, ask me before answering.
```

## FAQ

### Does it work the same in ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini?

Yes. The formula is about giving clear instructions, and that works in any assistant. Each one has its own answering style, so it's worth trying the same prompt in two tools and sticking with the one that suits you best.

### Does it work better in English?

Modern assistants understand and write very well in many languages. Write in the language you want the answer in.

### Is a longer prompt always better?

Not always. What matters is that it contains the information needed. A three-line prompt with a role, a task and a format is usually enough for simple tasks. For complex tasks, more context pays off.

### Do I need to be polite to the AI?

It's not required, but writing the way you'd talk to a person tends to produce clearer, more complete instructions. Clarity is what really matters.
